# Benchmarks

Methodology and recorded results. Numbers are machine-specific; every table
states its environment, and re-run instructions accompany each harness (see
[Methodology](./methodology.mdx)).

The charts on these pages render from the versioned JSONL that each rig appends
to `benchmarks/results/` (the record shape lives in `benchmarks/src/report.rs`).
Each metric carries its own unit and its own direction of goodness, so a chart
reads "higher is better" or "lower is better" from the data rather than from the
plotting code. Until a rig has been re-run under the current schema, its chart
shows a "no recorded data yet" placeholder. Every rendered chart also ships an
exact data table (its `<details>` fallback); the committed JSONL under
`benchmarks/results/` is the durable record.

## The two rigs, and the honest framing

In-process decode and operator costs are measured in isolation by the micro
and framework-overhead tiers; a full end-to-end pass sustains hundreds of
thousands of rows per second and the **sink** — not the decoder or the chain —
sets that rate. Both tiers exist precisely so a CPU-rig multiple is never
quoted as an end-to-end one: a several-fold decode win has repeatedly shrunk to
a fraction of a percent once a real broker and server are in the loop. Read
every micro figure as a ceiling on what the stage could contribute, not as a
throughput claim.

## Tiers

1. **Micro** — `cargo bench` under `crates/*/benches`: criterion in
   `spate-avro`, `spate-json` and `spate-clickhouse`, divan with an
   `AllocProfiler` in `spate-core`, and an instruction-count bench per crate
   measured under valgrind rather than a clock.
2. **Framework overhead** — `benchmarks/pipeline_synthetic` (release binary,
   JSON report): in-memory source → chain → null sink; no broker in the loop.
3. **At scale** — `benchmarks/e2e_kafka_clickhouse` + `benchmarks/loadgen`
   against local containers or external clusters (env-configured endpoints).

Weekly CI compiles every bench and rig, runs the wall-clock benches, smokes
three rigs that need no broker or container, and discards every timing: a hosted
runner cannot say whether a wall-clock number moved. What that tier establishes
is that nothing has stopped working.

The quantities that do hold on any machine are checked elsewhere. Allocation
counts on the chain hot path are asserted against fixed bounds by a test, on
every pull request that touches Rust. Instruction counts come from callgrind and
heap shape from DHAT, for the benched crates a change selects — its own crate,
or all of them for a change to the engine. Those numbers are advisory; what
gates is that the benches still build and run.
