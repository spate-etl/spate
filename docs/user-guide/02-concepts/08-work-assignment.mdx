# Work assignment

This page is the **source of truth for how coordinated work is
distributed**. [Source coordination](./07-source-coordination.mdx) covers
the substrate (splits, leases, fencing, quarantine, completion) and is
deliberately separate, because that substrate has outlived three different
assignment algorithms.

If the code and this page disagree, one of them is a bug. The
[invariants](#invariants) below name the tests that enforce them.

## The model

**One worker decides; every worker reconciles.**

The fleet elects a leader (a lease on a well-known key). The leader already
runs the source's planner; it also computes a **desired assignment** (a
map from instance to the splits that instance should hold) and publishes
one small record per instance at `assign.{instance}`. Every worker watches
its own record and moves toward it:

| Worker sees | Worker does |
|---|---|
| assigned, not held | claim it (lease write, then the record CAS) |
| held, still assigned | nothing — stickiness is free |
| held, no longer assigned | drain it cooperatively, then release |
| held, draining, assigned again | cancel the revocation; the drain finishes on its own |
| no record at all | hold what it has, claim nothing |

The last row states the rule. **Absence of an instruction is not an
instruction to hold nothing.** A worker that has never seen a record (a
cold start, a leader gap, a withdrawn assignment) keeps what it holds and
waits. Only a record that *exists and omits a split* means give it up.

### The store is the acknowledgment

There is no ack protocol between leader and worker, and no barrier. The
leader publishes the new assignment immediately, without waiting for the
previous owner to let go. What waits is the **claim**: the new owner cannot
take the split until the previous owner's lease key has disappeared, which
it observes directly through its watch, at its next poll on a store whose
watch is polled. An assignment is permission to try, not a promise the
split is free yet.

Other designs resolve the same dependency with an explicit acknowledgment:
a unit of work is only assigned to its new owner once the old owner has
confirmed the revocation. That acknowledgment is unnecessary here, because
the store already publishes the fact one would have carried: the lease key
is either there or it is not. No state is left that is visible to neither
side of a negotiation, so the leader does not have to order its writes
against the previous owner's.

### Reconciliation is continuous, not rounded

Workers converge independently and at their own pace. There are no
rebalance rounds and no global pause; a worker whose assignment did not
change never stops processing.

A round-based design computes revocations in one round and assignments in
the next, which assumes the member count does not change in between; a
worker that joins between the two leaves work stranded in a skew that never
self-corrects. Having no rounds means there is nothing to fall between.

## Contract

**A worker may assume:**

- Its assignment names only splits it is permitted to claim.
- A split absent from its assignment should be released, once a record
  exists at all.
- Nothing else. In particular an assignment is **not** a lease, **not** a
  fence, and **not** a promise the split is available yet, since the
  previous owner may still be draining it.

**The leader guarantees:**

- The assignment is total over the claimable pool, up to the fleet's total
  lane budget (`members × max_in_flight`). Splits above that budget are
  the queue, exactly as unleased splits are.
- No split is named in two instances' assignments.
- The computation is deterministic and idempotent in its inputs.

**Not guaranteed:**

- That the assignment is current. It is a decision published at a moment;
  membership may have changed since.
- That two workers cannot briefly both believe they hold a split. They can.
  **Assignment is not the fence**; the durable progress record's
  compare-and-swap is, and it is unchanged from the substrate page. A stale,
  split-brained, or wrong leader cannot produce two owners; it can only
  produce bad balance.

That last point is why this design needs no consensus algorithm. Raft was
rejected for this system on the grounds that the problem needs
linearizability only at the per-split commit; centralizing the *assignment*
does not weaken that argument, because the assignment carries no
correctness.

## Invariants

Each is enforced by a property test in
[`spate-coordination/src/protocol.rs`](repo:crates/spate-coordination/src/protocol.rs),
named here so a deleted or weakened test is visible as a divergence from this
page.

1. **Deterministic.** The same `(members, splits, ownership, reserved,
   last published assignment, lane budgets, seed)` always yields the same
   assignment. The seed keys the tie-breaks and is the *job* fingerprint,
   never the leader's identity. A leader-specific seed would re-break every
   tie on failover and churn the fleet for nothing. —
   `assignment_is_deterministic`
2. **No split assigned twice.** No split appears in two instances'
   assignments. — `no_split_is_assigned_twice`
3. **Stable under unchanged input.** Feeding the function's own output back
   as the last published assignment reproduces it exactly, while each
   split's claim in the leader's view matches that output or is absent; the
   assignment is a fixpoint. A lease from another owner that the split's
   progress record has moved past, or from a released split, is not a claim.
   A steady-state fleet therefore publishes nothing and drains nothing, and
   neither a claim on its way to the leader nor a lease whose deletion has
   not reached it moves anything. —
   `assignment_is_stable_under_unchanged_input`,
   `assignment_is_stable_under_unchanged_ownership`,
   `assignment_is_stable_before_its_claims_are_seen`,
   `assignment_is_stable_while_superseded_leases_linger`
4. **Locally optimal.** No single split can move to a member with lane
   budget and reduce imbalance. — `assignment_admits_no_improving_move`
5. **Total over the claimable pool.** Every assignable split is assigned
   unless every lane in the fleet is full. —
   `assignment_is_total_over_the_claimable_pool`

The improving-move pass is bounded by `MAX_IMPROVING_MOVES` as a termination
backstop. If that bound ever binds, the assignment stops being locally
optimal, and invariant 4's test is what says so.

## Balance objective

**Weight, not split count.**

Split count is a proxy for load, and a poor one for the sources this
framework coordinates: an object-store planner gives a large object it cannot
cut into ranges a split to itself, so two workers holding the same number of
splits can be holding wildly different numbers of bytes. The
planner already reports a weight per split; balancing uses it.

`max_in_flight` still caps the split *count* per worker. It is a lane
budget (a materialization limit), not a fairness knob.

Each worker advertises its **own** budget on its presence key and the
leader balances against that, so a fleet of unequally-sized pods works. The
alternative, a leader assuming its own value, strands work permanently: a
smaller worker looks least-loaded, keeps being assigned splits, and keeps
refusing to claim them.

Balancing on *counts* rather than weight is a trap: equal counts of unequal
work force the whole fleet to be provisioned for its worst-loaded member.
Balancing uses bytes rather than measured CPU because bytes are already
known at planning time and cost nothing to collect.

The passes:

1. **Sticky** — every split whose lease owner is a live member with a free
   lane stays with it. A lease counts while the split's progress record
   names the lease's owner, or while the lease is a claim ahead of the
   record. Leases and records reach the leader on separate watches, so a
   record can show another owner's later claim or a release while the lease
   it replaced is still in view, and that lease holds no lane. Each
   remaining split then stays with the first of these that is a live member
   with a free lane: the member the last published assignment named for it,
   and the owner on its progress record. Leased splits go first, so a split
   waiting for its claim cannot take a lane its lease owner still holds. A
   move costs a drain, so an assignment that churns for a marginally better
   balance is worse than one that does not. The last published assignment
   covers the time between an assignment and its claim reaching the leader's
   view: a store round trip, or up to about two poll intervals on a store
   whose watch is polled, and one more for each poll that reads data a
   recent write has not reached.
2. **Fill** — unassigned splits go to the least-loaded member with lane
   budget, heaviest split first (longest-processing-time greedy).
3. **Improve** — while a split can move from a heavier member to a lighter
   one and strictly reduce imbalance, move it.

Pass 3 admits a move exactly when `load(from) > load(to) + weight`, which
is the condition under which the move reduces the sum of squared loads.
Every accepted move strictly improves that potential, so the pass converges
and cannot oscillate.

## Rebalance delay

When an instance's presence key expires, its splits are **withheld** rather
than reassigned, for `rebalance_delay` (default 20s). A pod that comes back
inside the window reclaims its own work and nothing moves. The window is
cancelled the moment the instance reappears, so a fast restart pays
nothing.

The default is deliberately short. A long window suits systems whose work
units are expensive to start; a split here starts by reading a descriptor
and spawning a fetcher. When starting is cheap, idle work costs more than
movement does.

**`rebalance_delay: 0` means reassign immediately.** It is a supported
configuration, not a disabled feature, and it takes a distinct code path
rather than flowing through the delay logic with a zero comparison.
A delay knob whose zero flows through the general path as another
value is how "reassign at once" silently becomes "withhold indefinitely".
Enforced by
`a_zero_rebalance_delay_reassigns_immediately`.

## Revocation

A revocation is **cooperative first, forced second**.

The owner is asked to give the split up. A cooperating source stops intake
at a safe boundary, chases the split's tail to a final fenced commit, and
releases. The next owner then resumes from a watermark covering
everything the previous one emitted, so the transfer **replays nothing**.

A source that declines, or whose drain outruns `drain_deadline` (default
10s), has the release forced instead. The split still leaves; its
uncommitted tail replays under the next owner, bounded by one commit
interval. Declining is safe, but it is the expensive way to comply.

It is the same distinction any rebalancing protocol has to draw: a clean
release, where the owner flushes and commits, versus a fenced one, where it
has already lost the right to write and must discard.

**A revocation the leader takes back is cancelled.** If membership changes
again mid-drain and the leader names the split for its current owner once
more (while that owner still holds it), the pending forced release is
dropped and the revocation ends as
`revocations_total{outcome="cancelled"}`. The deadline exists to stop a
slow drain from pinning a *rebalance* open, and there is no longer a
rebalance to pin; forcing would charge one commit interval of replay for a
move that is no longer wanted. Cancelling therefore changes only the drain
that is slower than the deadline; a drain that would have finished inside
it was never going to be forced. Enforced by
`a_reassigned_split_cancels_its_own_revocation`.

**What cancelling removes is the replay, not the lane teardown**, and
only if the source had accepted. It takes these shapes:

- **The source had already stopped intake.** It keeps draining, so the
  split is still released and then re-claimed by the same worker on the
  next pass, one teardown and re-open, replaying nothing. Going further
  would mean asking a source to *resume* intake it has stopped at a safe
  boundary, and that is a seam
  [`SplitSource`](../06-extending/custom-source.mdx) deliberately does not
  have.
- **The source had declined**, or was never eligible. Nothing stopped, so
  nothing leaves: the split stays and keeps being read, with no teardown at
  all.

**A cancelled drain is still bounded, by silence and not by the deadline.**
Cancelling ends the revocation, and with it the deadline that served the
rebalance. It does not end the obligation to keep the split *readable*: a
source that stopped intake cannot be asked to resume, so a drain that never
finishes would leave the split owned, leased, assigned, and unread
for the life of the process, and a bounded job containing it could never
complete. So a cancelled drain that commits nothing for
`drain_deadline` has its release forced anyway, and the same worker
re-claims it with a fresh lane and starts reading, at a cost of one bounded
replay. A live drain commits as its tail acks its way to the sink, so this
fires only on a wedged one. Enforced by
`a_stalled_cancelled_drain_is_still_released`.

An absent assignment record cancels nothing. It means "nothing has been
decided", which is the last row of the table above read from the other
side.

`drain_deadline` may exceed `lease_duration`. A draining split is still
owned and still heartbeated, so a long drain does not race its own lease;
it only makes the rebalance slow. Size it against how long the source needs
to flush a split's tail through its sink, which for a paced sink can be
minutes. The only floor is `op_timeout`: a deadline below one store
round-trip would force every revocation before its final commit could
land.

## Discovery

On a store that pushes changes, every worker watches both keyspaces whole
and checks its view against a full listing every `reconcile_interval`.

A store whose watch is polled lists what each watch covers once per
interval, and a key written and removed between two listings never reaches
the watch. On such a store:

- Every worker watches the lease keyspace whole, and of the durable
  keyspace only the assignment records, the plan record and the verdict
  marker.
- A worker reads the records of a split it is assigned and has not seen,
  and claims it once they are in its view.
- The leader re-reads, every poll interval, each split it assigned that is
  runnable and shows no lease, so a completion, failure or quarantine whose
  lease it never saw still reaches it. It reads the spec of every split it
  has seen without one.
- Only the leader reconciles, and only the split records. That listing
  brings in a split written without passing through its view, such as a
  deposed leader's late seeding.
- A new leader lists every split and spec record before it plans or
  publishes. Until that listing lands it writes no assignment, so no worker
  is told to give up a split the leader has not seen yet.
- A worker that reports the job terminal writes a verdict marker. A worker
  that sees the marker lists the split records and judges from that
  listing, since its own view may not cover the plan.

On a store whose expiry is judged by each observer, a peer judges a lease
expired at the first poll that starts one lease after the peer first read the
lease's current revision. A watching peer sees a dead worker's lease expire up
to one lease plus two poll intervals after the last heartbeat, plus the time
its polls spend reading. A peer that starts watching late sees a stale lease
expire one lease after its first read of it, plus up to one poll interval. A
worker claims a split only once the split's lease has expired in its own view,
so a worker that started watching after the death takes over a split assigned
to it no sooner than that.

## Failure behavior

| Failure | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Leader dies | No rebalancing until re-election (one lease). Held work continues; assignments are durable and survive the gap. There is **no leaderless balancing fallback** — one mechanism, not two. On a store whose watch is polled, the new leader lists the split records before its first assignment. |
| Worker dies | Presence key expires; after `rebalance_delay` the leader reassigns. Split leases expire independently, so the work is claimable. |
| Worker wedged but alive | Its lease stops being renewed and expires, and its presence key with it; the leader withholds its splits for `rebalance_delay` and then assigns them elsewhere. The expiry makes the split *claimable*; the assignment is still what makes someone claim it. |
| Assignment write lost | The worker keeps its previous assignment. The leader republishes on its next step. |
| Source refuses to drain | Forced revocation as soon as the decline lands, or after `drain_deadline` if it never does; bounded replay. If the revocation was cancelled first, the refusal costs nothing — the split was staying anyway. |
| Drain wedges after its revocation was cancelled | Released after `drain_deadline` with no commit landing, then re-claimed by the same worker with a fresh lane; bounded replay. Without this the split would be owned and unread. |

## What this is not

- **Not consensus.** The leader is cheap and may be wrong. Correctness
  lives in the record CAS.
- **Not a fence.** See the contract above.
- **Not exactly-once.** Forced revocations and takeovers duplicate; they
  never lose.
- **Not work stealing.** Workers do not select work for themselves and
  cannot take a split from a peer.
  [ADR-0038](../../adr/0038-leader-computed-sticky-assignment.md) records
  why, superseding [ADR-0023](../../adr/0023-dynamic-work-stealing-coordination.md).
