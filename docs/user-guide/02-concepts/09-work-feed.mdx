# Work feed

A work feed is a queue of [work units](../07-reference/glossary.mdx#work-unit)
that a source reads its input through. Each unit names a piece of input, such
as an object to read, and the source decodes the unit's body to find it. The
[feed](../07-reference/glossary.mdx#feed) hands a unit to a reader, and the
reader acknowledges it once the unit's records are safely downstream. A unit
carries no progress. It is acknowledged once, as a whole, or delivered again
and read again from its start.

A feed sits behind the `Feed` trait, so any queue that can hold a unit, hide
it while a reader holds it, and deliver it again can back one. The in-process
feed serves a single instance and keeps nothing across a restart.

## The ack rule

Every feed and every source follows one rule:

- A source acknowledges a unit only after it has read the unit to its end and
  the sink has acknowledged every record in it.
- A unit read in several parts is acknowledged once, after every part meets
  that rule.
- A unit whose input was replaced while it was read may be acknowledged once
  a unit naming the input's current state is stored in the feed and the sink
  has acknowledged every record read from it. Past the source's re-enqueue
  cap it is acknowledged without one, and the source counts it.
- Otherwise, a unit whose read stops before its end leaves the feed through
  redelivery, until the feed moves it to its dead-letter destination.

The rule is the unit-level form of the
[at-least-once guarantee](02-delivery-guarantees.mdx). A source learns that
the sink has acknowledged a unit's last record the same way it learns any
committable position: the acknowledged watermark of the partition the unit is
read on reaches the offset one past that record. A unit that yields no records
has nothing to wait for and is acknowledged once its read ends.

## Redelivery and duplicates

A feed delivers each unit at least once. Every delivery carries a new
receipt, and a source keys the units it holds by receipt:

- **A failed unit is delivered again.** A source that cannot read a unit, for
  example because the input it names is missing or undecodable, fails it back
  to the feed.
- **A leased unit returns when its renewals stop.** A source renews every unit
  it holds from its control plane. A feed that leases units keeps a unit from
  other readers only while it is renewed, so a control plane that stops
  running gives its units back for another reader.
- **A duplicate is read again in full.** A second delivery of a unit the
  source already holds is read again from its start, beside the first. No
  feed deduplicates by unit id.
- **A unit a stopping worker hands back is delivered again.** On a graceful
  stop, a source on a feed that hands back returns each unit it has not read to
  its end, and another reader reads it from its start, at once on a feed that
  leases units and after the worker closes the feed on one whose positions move
  only by commit. A handed-back unit is not a failure
  ([Graceful shutdown](../03-guides/graceful-shutdown.mdx#the-drain-choreography)).

A call with a receipt the feed no longer honors, because the unit was
delivered again or already settled, answers `Gone`. The source lets go of that
delivery, and the newer one carries the unit from there.

Each redelivery repeats records already sent downstream, so a destination
that cannot tolerate duplicates needs the replay-safe design
[delivery guarantees](02-delivery-guarantees.mdx) describes.

## Dead-lettering

A feed counts the deliveries of each unit. A unit that reaches the feed's
delivery cap without an acknowledgment moves to its dead-letter destination and
is not delivered again, except in a window its backend's page describes. A unit
that fails on every reader, such as one naming an object that no longer exists,
ends there after a bounded number of attempts. A feed that counts only the
deliveries its reader reported as failed does not count one whose reader stopped
first, so a unit that stops every reader is delivered again without bound. Each
backend's page says which it counts. The in-process feed keeps its dead letters
in memory, with exact counts of the units it holds in each state.

Dead-lettering is a property of the feed. The
[record-level error policies](04-error-handling.mdx) still decide what happens
to an individual record that fails inside a unit that is read.

## How a bounded job ends

A job whose `feed:` section names a job location is bounded: a lister run
publishes its units once, and the job ends when every one of them is read. No
worker can tell that from its own units, since a peer may hold a unit the feed
delivers again if that peer stops, and a feed's counts can be estimates. A
bounded job ends on three checks, in order:

1. The end marker the lister run writes at the job location names the same
   run at the start and at the end of a settling window, five minutes unless
   [`settling_window`](../07-reference/configuration.mdx#feed) sets another.
2. Over the window, every read of the feed, at most 20 seconds apart, finds no
   unit ready, delayed or held by any reader, and the worker holds none
   itself. A read that finds one, a read that fails and may succeed later, a
   read that comes late or a unit the worker takes starts the window again. A
   read the feed or the job location refuses for good, such as for a missing
   permission, fails the run.
3. The feed's dead-letter destination is empty. When it holds units the job
   is stalled: it fails with an error that includes `bounded feed job stalled:`
   and names the destination and its count, and the process exits non-zero.

Every worker runs the check and keeps receiving until it passes, idle or not,
so a unit a stopped peer held still has a reader when the feed delivers it
again. A solo run on the in-process feed the runtime built has exact counts and
its own lister, so it ends once its listing is read and the feed is empty, with
no window. A run given an in-process feed in code runs until a shutdown, since
code may publish to it after the listing.

A unit the feed itself deletes before anyone reads it, at the end of the feed's
retention, leaves no trace, and the job can end over it. A bounded job cannot
share its feed with storage notifications: notifications keep the window from
closing, and an object both announce is read twice.

## How the control plane waits

The control plane drives the feed synchronously, and no call to the feed waits
on I/O. A feed backend runs its receives, lease renewals and publishes on a
task or thread of its own. A receive returns what that task has already
buffered, and the other calls hand their request to it. The control plane
therefore never blocks on the feed, in keeping with the rule that
[a source thread never blocks](03-backpressure.mdx).

When the control plane has nothing to do, it waits on a signal the feed raises
when a unit becomes ready, for at most its poll timeout. A source can also hand
that signal to its lanes, so a lane that reaches the end of its input or cannot
read its unit ends the wait at once. A wait consumes the signal, and a receive
that takes nothing raises none, so the control plane receives what it has room
for before it waits. A source holds a bounded number of units at once and
receives only while it holds fewer.

## Who provisions the feed

The operator provisions a feed's queues, topics and dead-letter destinations,
and the notification rules that publish to it. The framework creates none of
them. At startup the feed reads the settings it depends on and refuses a
destination it cannot read. A backend that can compare retention also refuses a
dead-letter destination that would expire a unit before the source queue does. A
bounded job uses a queue and a dead-letter destination of its own. Each feed
backend's page lists its checks and the permissions its roles need.
